# The 4C Model

## 1. Purpose

The 4C model is a plain-language way to describe how meaning can be produced through traversal.

It is not presented as a complete theory of language, cognition, or transformer internals. It is a working model for separating four things that are often collapsed into the single claim that a model “understood” something.

The four Cs are:

1. Composition
2. Compilation
3. Connotation
4. Construal

Together, they describe a path from structured material to an adopted meaning.

## 2. The Basic Idea

A model does not encounter meaning as a finished object.

It encounters a composition: words, nodes, relationships, frames, and other structures arranged in some order.

The model reads through that composition. This reading is called compilation.

As it reads, the composition activates and reshapes a field of associations, expectations, and possible interpretations. This is connotation.

At some point, usually in response to a task, the model behaves as though one interpretation is the meaning that matters here. This is construal.

In plain English:

A composition is read, that reading develops a connotation, and the model commits to a construal.

## 3. Composition

Composition is the structured material available to be read.

In a graph traversal, composition includes the nodes touched along the path, the relationships between them, the order in which they are encountered, and any active frame or context.

Composition is not merely a list of ingredients. Order and arrangement matter.

The same nodes encountered in a different order may form a different effective composition because earlier material changes how later material is understood.

A composition may include:

- lexical forms;
- concepts;
- operators;
- roles;
- events;
- states;
- modalities;
- reference frames;
- previously compiled structures;
- the sequence in which these are encountered.

Composition answers the question:

What has been placed together for the model to read?

## 4. Compilation

Compilation is the model reading through the composition.

In this model, compilation is not a separate semantic object. It is the process by which the model attends to the material, follows the path, and incorporates each part into what has already been read.

Compilation is path-dependent.

The model does not read every node in isolation. Earlier parts of the traversal affect how later parts are interpreted. The meaning of the current step depends partly on the route taken to reach it.

Compilation may be affected by:

- order;
- framing;
- repetition;
- emphasis;
- omitted alternatives;
- traversal depth;
- the task being performed;
- the model’s existing training and context.

Compilation answers the question:

How was the composition read?

## 5. Connotation

Connotation is the changing field of associations and possible meanings produced during compilation.

The model begins with tendencies inherited from training. A term such as `burn` may initially suggest heat, fire, injury, cooking, exercise, insult, destruction, or other familiar associations.

Traversal does not erase those associations and replace them with a stored definition. It progressively channels and refines them.

As the model moves through a path, some interpretations become more salient and others become less relevant.

For example, a path through verbal use, an event, thermal damage, and a medical frame makes injury increasingly available as a likely reading. A path through possibility, harmful outcomes, and a safety frame makes hazard increasingly available.

Connotation is not necessarily one answer. It is the interpretive field from which an answer may be selected.

Connotation answers the question:

What does this reading now suggest?

## 6. Construal

Construal is the interpretation the model ultimately adopts for the present purpose.

The commitment may be explicit, as in a definition or classification. It may also be implicit, revealed by the way the model continues reasoning, chooses relevant details, predicts consequences, or generates an answer.

Construal is task-dependent.

The same connotative field may lead to different construals when the model is asked to explain, classify, summarize, compare, continue a sentence, or make a decision.

Construal does not have to mean that all ambiguity has disappeared. A model can construe a composition as ambiguous, conditional, or open to several readings. That is still a construal of what the composition means in the present task.

Construal answers the question:

What is this being taken to mean here?

## 7. Path Dependence

The 4C model treats meaning as path-dependent.

Two traversals may end at the same concept while producing different connotations because they arrived there through different structures.

A path that reaches injury through an event and bodily damage carries a different history from a path that reaches injury through a nominal result.

The endpoint alone does not preserve that difference.

This retained effect of the route is called semantic hysteresis.

Semantic hysteresis means that what a model takes from the current point depends partly on what it has already encountered.

The path is therefore part of the meaning-producing process, not merely a route used to retrieve a destination.

## 8. The Relationship Between the Four Cs

The four Cs are not four identical stages or four independent modules.

Composition is the material.

Compilation is the reading process.

Connotation is the evolving interpretive effect of that process.

Construal is the adopted meaning.

The relationship can be stated plainly:

1. A composition is assembled.
2. The model compiles it by reading through it.
3. The reading develops and refines a connotation.
4. The model construes the composition for the task at hand.

Connotation develops during compilation rather than waiting until compilation is complete.

## 9. Intervention Points

The 4C model can be used to identify where an intervention is being made.

A composition intervention changes what is present or how it is arranged. This may include adding a frame, removing a node, changing the path, or reordering material.

A compilation intervention changes how the composition is encountered. This may include pacing, chunking, repetition, emphasis, visibility, or traversal policy.

A connotation intervention changes the background associations that become available. This may occur through prior context, examples, training, model choice, or contrastive framing. In ordinary use, this layer is usually influenced indirectly through composition and compilation.

A construal intervention changes the conditions of commitment. This may include asking for one answer, requesting several interpretations, specifying a perspective, requiring justification, or allowing ambiguity to remain unresolved.

The intervention question is:

Which part of the meaning pathway is being changed, by whom, and for what purpose?

## 10. Using the Model as a Lens

The 4C model can be applied to a term, message, event, image, situation, or graph traversal.

Begin with composition:

What was present, how was it arranged, and in what order was it encountered?

Then examine compilation:

How did the reader move through it, and what earlier material affected later material?

Then examine connotation:

What interpretations, associations, expectations, or evaluations became more or less available during the reading?

Finally, examine construal:

What did the reader or model ultimately behave as though the composition meant?

This lens can also be used comparatively. Two readers may encounter different compositions, compile the same composition differently, develop different connotations, or commit to different construals.

## 11. Simple Example

Consider the reply:

`Fine.`

The composition includes the word, the punctuation, the preceding exchange, the timing of the reply, and the relationship between the speakers.

Compilation is the recipient reading the reply after the preceding exchange and noticing its brevity and punctuation.

Connotation may include agreement, annoyance, resignation, reluctance, or dismissal.

Construal is the interpretation the recipient adopts, such as:

The person agrees, but is unhappy about it.

A different surrounding composition could produce a different construal of the same word.

## 12. The 4C Model in One Paragraph

The 4C model describes meaning as the result of a path-dependent reading process. A composition of words, nodes, relations, frames, and ordering is compiled by a model. During that reading, the model’s existing associations are activated and progressively refined into a connotative field. For the purpose of the current task, the model then commits, explicitly or implicitly, to a construal of what the composition means. The model is useful because it separates what was presented, how it was read, what it came to suggest, and what it was ultimately taken to mean.
